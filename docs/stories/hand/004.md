The warmth of the bar, the smell of wood and ozone, the solid floor beneath her—it all felt like a sick joke. The adrenaline that had carried her through the fight, the climb, the explosion, curdled in her veins, replaced by a cold, shaking fury.

Damien's hand was still extended, his expression one of mild, analytical interest. *An interesting solution.*

The words were the spark.

Elara ignored his hand. She pushed herself to her feet, every bruise and strained muscle screaming. The shield, that cursed piece of metal, was still in her grip, warm from the cataclysmic energies it had just channeled. She looked from it to Damien, who was taking another infuriatingly calm sip from his mug.

"**You.**" she said, her voice a low, ragged thing. "**You could have taken it back at any time. You could make a thousand of them. You sent me into that... that meat grinder *for a test*.**"

Damien lowered his mug. "**You learned—**"

"**I learned that your 'gift' is a death sentence!**" she shouted, the sound raw in the quiet bar. Lorain stopped polishing her glass. Xeroth, the chinchilla, froze mid-groom. "**Those weren't just guards, they were fanatics! They saw your mark and they didn't just want to arrest me, they wanted to *dissect* me! To *purge* me! Kaelen was terrified of *me* because of it! The mission wasn't about saving her, it was about seeing how long I could last with a target painted on my back by the King of Chaos himself!**"

She was trembling now, the full weight of the near-death experiences, the terror, the manipulation crashing down. "**You watched. You *knew*. And you let it happen. You made me go back for this... this *thing*!**" She hefted the shield.

With a guttural cry of pure frustration, she threw it. Not with warrior's precision, but with all the pent-up rage of a used tool. It spun across the bar, towards the space near his feet, clattering and skidding to a stop against the leg of his stool.

The chaotic crest gave one final, feeble pulse and went dark.

Silence, thicker than the castle's, filled the room.

Elara didn't wait for a response. She turned on her heel and marched toward the shadow that led to her quarters. Her back was straight, her steps measured, a knight's discipline holding her together just long enough to escape.

She heard Xeroth's tiny, whispered, "**Ooh, dramatic exit. Seven out of ten.**"

She didn't hear Damien say anything at all.

The castle corridor welcomed her with its profound silence, which now felt less like peace and more like a tomb. She slammed the door to her room—a satisfying, solid *thud* that echoed once before being swallowed—and stood in the center of the austere space, breathing hard.

The fury held for a few more minutes, a hot, sustaining fire. Then it guttered out, leaving behind the ashes: exhaustion, shame, and a deep, chilling understanding.

She had thrown the shield at a primordial being who could unmake galaxies. She had shouted at the Eternal King in his own domain. And he had... let her. He hadn't struck her down. He hadn't even seemed offended. He had watched her with those ancient, golden eyes, as if observing a fascinating chemical reaction.

That was worse.

She sank onto the edge of the bed, her head in her hands. The mission replayed in her mind not as heroism, but as a series of traps. The shield was a trap. The order to retrieve it was a trap. Her own loyalty, her own stubbornness, had been the bait.

He was remaking her. Sanding down the edges of the knight, exposing the raw, pragmatic core beneath. And he was doing it by putting her in situations where every noble instinct led to a dead end, and the only way out was to think like *him*.

She stayed there for a long time. The nebula outside her window cycled through hues of violet and deep blue. No one came. No summons, no ghostly voice in her mind. Just the waiting silence.

She knew, with a certainty that was its own kind of prison, that she would have to go back. She couldn't hide in this barracks-room forever. There was no food here, no water unless the castle provided it. She was reliant on his hospitality even in her defiance.

And beneath the anger, a treacherous part of her was... curious. What had he seen in her reaction? What was the point of that brutal, final lesson? What did he want the tool to become?

Hours passed. The fury settled into a hard, cold knot in her stomach. The shame faded, replaced by a weary resolve. She was a soldier. The mission was over. Debriefing was required, even if the commanding officer was a capricious god.

She stood, straightened her tunic—the one he had provided—and looked at herself in the dark glass of the window. A woman with shadows under her eyes, wearing the clothes of her captor, gearing up to face the being who held her leash.

She had no shield to carry this time. She wasn't sure if that made her weaker, or finally, honestly, herself.

Taking a deep breath of the silent, sterile air, she turned and opened the door to face the King.

Back in the bar, the shield lay where it had fallen. Lorain had come around from behind the bar and picked it up, running a cloth over the smudged surface. "**She's got a point, you know.**" she said, not looking at Damien. "**You could have just summoned it back. Or made a new one. That was cruel, even for you.**"

Damien finally moved, setting his now-empty mug on the bar. He looked at the shield in Lorain's hands, his expression unreadable. "**Cruelty would have been letting her die, Sparrow. Or leaving her in that city to be dissected by the Inquisitors. What I did was... instructive.**"

"**Almost getting her killed is instructive?**" Lorain placed the shield gently on the bar.

"***Almost* is the key word.**" Damien said, a faint, real smile touching his lips. "**She was never in true danger of death. Not while I was watching. But she needed to believe she was. She needed to feel the weight of the mark as a burden, not just a weapon. To understand that bearing my sigil means walking a line where some will worship and others will burn everything to purge you.**"

He reached out and traced the edge of the crest with a fingertip. It glimmered faintly at his touch. "**She threw it. Not in surrender. In defiance. She claimed her anger. That is progress. The knight would have borne the insult in stoic silence. The weapon I am forging... the weapon she might choose to become... will know when to roar.**"

Xeroth, now a small, sleek ferret draped over a bottle, yawned. "**So, what now? Wait for her to stop sulking?**"

Damien's golden eyes drifted toward the shadowed archway leading to the castle. "**Now,**" he said softly, "**we see if she comes back. And what she says when she does. That will be the most interesting lesson of all.**"
